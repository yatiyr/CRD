## Read for this batch

Read 2026-11-02:

  56cf7f40c41b2566bfe9c3df18e73cd649b7c0e33690439a43582a65a3b3a0cc): sections 4 to 7, 10 and 11. The final RFC keeps the

  b1bee06a814f92ca4677c85b97519be53869b2bf8edc79ade955fb51c0402b17): appendix A.1, which lets a client send an alert
  about the server's flight in the clear (OpenSSL's `ech_required` below).
- IANA's HPKE registry (`hpke.xml`, SHA-256 9d0848b4152439cfbc9a8fa8fb0cc36c1879844db7fd2e4f4c706b1c66d03002): the KEM,
  KDF and AEAD identifiers `tls.ech` now records, and the post-quantum KEMs (ML-KEM-768 0x0041, X-Wing) it tracks.

## The peer

OpenSSL 3.5 has no ECH; 4.0 added it. `openssl` in cmake/pins.json is now 4.0.3 (released 2026-09-29), fetched from
openssl.org, 55,235,667 bytes, SHA-256 325b5c806167c13b40b1ffeadfe0248197c00eccc4cf123ec1e28d2d2fd216d9, the same as
openssl.org's `.sha256` and the GitHub release's, LICENSE.txt Apache-2.0. The peer builds under a prefix named by the
version, so a new pin builds afresh (the old stamps kept 3.5.9's libraries under the new sources). OpenSSL 4 hands out
certificate names read-only: the peer's certificate builds its subject apart. All 57 earlier interop cases pass against
4.0.3 unchanged.

## What landed

- **`TlsEchKey`, `tls_ech_config_list`** and `TlsConfig::ech_keys`, `previous_ech_keys`, `ech_configs`, `ech_grease`;
  `TlsError::EchRejected`, `TlsEch` (`ech()`, `tls_ech_name`) and `ech_retry_configs()`; the `ech_retry_configs`
  argument of `TlsSession::init` and `QuicHandshake::init`.
- A client given a list must see the provider encrypt an inner ClientHello before its first flight leaves, or the
  session is cleared and init() returns BadConfig: AWS-LC itself proceeds without ECH when no configuration is usable.
- A server with a name answers its keys' public names only for a rejected client's recovery and refuses a handshake
  that completes under one.
- Interop peers: ECH options in `crd-tls-peer` (`--ech-configs`, `--ech-retry`, `--ech-grease`, `--ech-key`,
  `--ech-previous`, `--ech-config`) and `crd-openssl-tls-peer` (`--ech-configs`, `--ech-grease`, `--ech-pem`,
  `--ech-previous-pem`, `--ech-generate`), which also prints OpenSSL's error queue when its client cannot start.

## Found on the way

- OpenSSL 4.0.3's client cannot offer ECH over its QUIC TLS interface: it adds the transport parameters as a custom
  extension, which its ECH cannot copy into the outer hello (`custom_ext_add: bad extension`, internal_error, nothing
  sent). The interop covers QUIC ECH from a Cerid client to OpenSSL's server; a Cerid server's QUIC ECH is proven
  against Cerid clients.
- Rejected by a Cerid server, OpenSSL's client sends `ech_required` before it installs its handshake keys, in the clear
  (RFC 9846 appendix A.1 allows it); AWS-LC answers the unprotected record with bad_record_mac. Both ends fail, the
  retry configurations reach OpenSSL and its retry is accepted; the harness accepts either form of the ending.
- `crd-fuzz-crypto-quic` (batch 41) had no `scripts/fuzz.py` entry, which test-repository-tools' driver checks
  caught; main fixed it meanwhile (9a21f48c). Its test-instruments row now names its tokens and 23 seeds, and the TLS
  target's row its QUIC and ECH modes.
- AWS-LC sends the application protocols in the outer hello and has no GREASE `pre_shared_key` there (RFC 9849 section
  6.1.2's SHOULD); recorded under S14 as what the outer hello still shows.
